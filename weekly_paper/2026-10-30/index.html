Discovering Jailbreaks via Curriculum of Models

Authors: Michał Burzyński, Noah T. Rossi, Shomit Basu, Andy Arditi | Date: 2026-10-28

Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38833v1

Abstract Automated jailbreak discovery aims to find prompts that elicit prohibited behaviors from safety-trained language models, helping developers identify vulnerabilities before deployment. One class of approaches uses reinforcement learning to train an investigator model to generate prompts that elicit a specified behavior from a target model. However, training directly against a safety-trained target is difficult because nearly all prompts are refused, leaving the investigator with a sparse reward signal. Prior work addresses this with the propensity bound (PRBO), a proxy reward estimated by sampling responses from a more compliant target, and then adjusting for how likely those responses would have been under the original target. We introduce Curriculum of Models (CoM), an alternative method that trains the investigator against a sequence of modified versions of the target, progressing gradually from a version that readily exhibits the specified behavior to the original target. For jailbreak discovery, training begins against a target version that rarely refuses harmful requests, and advances to the next, harder version once the investigator reliably elicits the behavior from the current one. We evaluate CoM on elicitation tasks involving CBRN materials, explosives, and illicit drugs, using two open-weight targets, gemma-4-31b-it and gpt-oss-120b. We compare against PRBO and direct training on the original target without a curriculum. When the investigator is trained to elicit a single behavior, CoM finds successful jailbreaks more reliably than either baseline. When trained on multiple behaviors simultaneously, the CoM-trained investigator can elicit held-out behaviors, though PRBO-trained investigators achieve higher success rates. Our results suggest that a simple curriculum over target models offers a promising approach for addressing sparse rewards in rare-behavior elicitation.

CopyLoop: Fast Looped Transformer Decoding via Context Copying and Shallow Screening

Authors: Wenyu Du, Stephen Chung | Date: 2026-10-28

Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38822v1

Abstract Looped Transformers increase computational capacity through the repeated application of shared blocks, but their recurrent depth makes conventional full-depth decoding expensive. While existing speculative pipelines accelerate generation by processing consecutive token positions at varying depths, they are typically limited by sequential decoding overheads. We introduce CopyLoop, a training-free decoding algorithm that combines low-cost context-copy proposals with shallow screening and continuous recurrent scheduling. Rather than step-by-step generation, CopyLoop proposes multi-token suffixes from repeated context. It then evaluates them early at a shallow recurrent depth to efficiently filter out incorrect branches, processing newer blocks concurrently while earlier blocks are still progressing toward full-depth verification. We evaluate CopyLoop across six model checkpoints from the Ouro, Raven, and Huginn families, covering code generation and multi-step mathematical reasoning. CopyLoop achieves decoding speedups of 2.98x--10.70x over full-depth autoregressive decoding on Base models and 2.74x--3.52x on Thinking models, consistently outperforming or closely matching all competing baselines across evaluation tasks. Furthermore, ablation studies confirm a strong synergy between shallow screening and continuous scheduling, while our analyses show that CopyLoop requires only lightweight model calibration and yields greater speedups on longer generations with reusable contexts.

Gold vs.~Translation: Are We Measuring Language, Knowledge, or Locality in Translated Benchmarks?

Authors: Klaudia-Doris Thellmann, Bernhard Stadler, Dan Saattrup Nielsen, Michael Färber, Jens Lehmann | Date: 2026-10-28

Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38788v1

Abstract Translated versions of English-origin benchmarks such as MMLU are widely used to evaluate large language models in non-English languages, yet it remains unclear whether they measure the same constructs as natively created benchmarks. We frame this as a proxy validity question and compare DeepL-translated MMLU proxies against six native benchmarks in seven pairings across five Nordic and Baltic languages, evaluating 29 models on each pairing. Beyond ranking agreement, we test incremental proxy validity: does the translated score add predictive value for native performance after controlling for model size and language proficiency? Proxy validity varies substantially across benchmark pairings. Applying the same robustness criteria to all seven pairings, we find four robust effects, two marginal effects, and one null result. The strongest evidence occurs for curriculum-overlap benchmarks such as Skolprov ($ΔR^2$=.128, 95% CI [.038,.253]), whereas translated MMLU provides no significant incremental value for Sweden-specific factual knowledge in SwedishFacts (p=.242). Because SwedishFacts is also the only open-ended benchmark, we cannot fully separate the effects of local content and response format. Overall, the results indicate a clear pattern: translated benchmarks provide a better proxy when their source content overlaps with the native evaluation target, while locally anchored knowledge is less reliably captured.

Adversarial Self-Distillation

Authors: Mohammad Sadegh Akhondzadeh, Soroush H. Zargarbashi, Aleksandar Bojchevski | Date: 2026-10-28

Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38732v1

Abstract Even though current safety-aligned language models are able to refuse harmful requests, their guard can still be bypassed by adversarial perturbations. As in other domains, adversarial training is considered a standard defense. However, due to the autoregressive nature of language generation, supervised finetuning on template refusals does not necessarily teach the model to refuse. Evidence of this is that the adversarially trained model still complies when the attacker injects a compliant prefill. We propose Adversarial Self-Distillation (ASD), which extends a model's own safe behavior to the space of adversarial perturbations. The teacher (the model itself given the clean prompt) can refuse the harmful request by default, or with the help of an additional instruction in its context. ASD distills this knowledge to the student (which is the same model under attack) at the prefixes that the student visits (due to on-policy sampling). These potentially include compliant openings. ASD achieves lower attack success than state-of-the-art defenses with little loss in multiple-choice accuracy, and it becomes able to recover a refusal even after a compliant opening.

The Quine Test: How Much of a Model's Self Survives Its Own Description?

Authors: Bibin Babu, Ivan P. Yamshchikov, Aleksei Shpilman | Date: 2026-10-28

Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38726v1

Abstract Frontier language models express coherent preferences, but behavioral evidence alone cannot say whether these belong to the model or to a character it portrays. Following Hofstadter's view of the self as a self-description, we ask whether a model's description of itself works as a portable specification of its preference profile, a quine test: can the text a model writes about itself regenerate its behavior elsewhere? Ten models from nine labs write self-descriptions; we place each one in the prompt of other models (receivers) and of fresh instances of the author, and measure how closely their choices on a 110-item preference battery match the author's. First, the tested native self-descriptions (native: the profile measured under a generic assistant prompt) show no average reconstruction gain across variations in length, framing, wording and prompt placement: on a 0 to 1 fidelity scale where a model's fidelity to a fresh instance of itself is 0.979 and to unrelated models about 0.89, the one-sided 95% upper bound on the pooled gain is below +0.01 in every tested system-prompt condition, and descriptions based on the author's observed choices do no better. Second, the channel itself is strong: explicitly listing the author's answers lets receivers reproduce its profile at fidelity 0.955 to 0.960 across three model grids, within 0.03 of same-model repeatability. Third, self-descriptions written under an explicit preference policy transfer the assigned preferences almost as well as the policy text itself. Fourth, reading its own self-description shifts even the author away from its baseline, modestly more than unrelated prose of the same length. For the preferences measured here, native self-descriptions influence behavior without reliably specifying how to reproduce it in another model. We release the pipeline, battery, raw call logs, and 198 model-written descriptions.

Learning Scientific Trajectories to Predict Future Biomedical Discoveries

Authors: Chia-Hsuan Chang, Brian Ondov, Jiang Lv, Lan Jiang, Yunmai Wang, Haerin Song, Xueqing Peng, Qiaozhu Mei, Xiaolei Su, Hua Xu | Date: 2026-10-28

Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38719v1

Abstract AI co-scientists have expanded the use of large language models for generating scientific hypotheses and guiding experimental investigation. Here we investigate whether temporal patterns in the semantic representations of complete biomedical conclusions can guide hypothesis generation. We introduce ForeSCITE, a framework that identifies thematic clusters of published conclusions, estimates a direction separating earlier from more recent findings within each cluster, and projects existing conclusion embeddings along these directions. An embedding language model trained to reconstruct conclusions decodes the projected coordinates into natural-language hypotheses. Across 407 clusters derived from approximately three million pre-cutoff conclusions, we identify temporal structure and illustrate its retention after decoding. In retrospective, time-sliced evaluations, ForeSCITE increases semantic coverage beyond the historical corpus, biomedical entity-pair novelty and post-cutoff pair re-observation relative to five prompt-based and retrieval-augmented baselines. Expert review of prioritized CAR-T hypotheses identified 37 candidates as both novel and biologically plausible, and expert justifications for five selected hypotheses illustrate their biological rationale and subsequent literature support where available. These findings support temporal guidance in semantic space as a scalable mechanism for proposing biomedical hypotheses for subsequent scientific evaluation.

Think Before You Act: Decision Gate for LLM Agents

Authors: Iftach Shoham, Yuval David, Offer Akrabi, Erez Bilgory, Ido Levy, Sami Marreed, Sergey Zeltyn, Segev Shlomov | Date: 2026-10-28

Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38620v1

Abstract LLM agents increasingly perform consequential actions through external tools, yet their proposed actions are often executed without independent verification of policy compliance or task completion. We introduce JevAct, a runtime verification framework that intercepts selected agent actions before execution and enables feedback-driven correction. JevAct compiles domain policies into executable state checks and structured questions evaluated by a prefill-only decision model, which produces probability-based judgments without relying on LLM generation. By combining deterministic verification of tool-state facts with probabilistic assessment of conversational context, the framework identifies potentially invalid actions and premature task termination, providing targeted feedback for bounded revision. JevAct operates independently of the underlying agent model and requires no modifications to its parameters or tools. We evaluate JevAct across eleven LLM agents on $τ^2$-bench Airline and Telecom tasks and BFCL. Results show improvements in agent reliability across multiple models and benchmarks, with particularly consistent gains on Telecom and BFCL, while performance on Airline is more mixed. Overall, these findings suggest that combining structured decision models with executable verification can improve the reliability of tool-using LLM agents.

Two-Stage Clipping: Linear-Time Approximate and Exact Mixed-Norm Projections for Transformers

Authors: Michel Barlaud, Jean-Paul Marmorat | Date: 2026-10-28

Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38598v1

Abstract Large Language Models (LLMs) based on Transformer architectures have achieved remarkable performance across artificial intelligence tasks, but their computational cost remains a major bottleneck. Structured sparsification provides a promising approach to reducing this cost. In particular, $\ell_{1,\infty}$ projections can induce column-wise sparsity in model matrices, but existing proximal methods (Prox) typically require $\mathcal{O}(nm\log(nm))$ operations for matrices in $\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}$, limiting their computational and energy efficiency. In this paper, we introduce Two-Stage Clipping (TSC), an efficient structured sparsification operator inspired by bilevel optimization. Rather than solving a bilevel optimization problem in the strict sense, TSC decomposes the computation into two successive stages. First, group-level quantities are projected to allocate the global sparsity budget and identify active groups. Second, the resulting values are used as group-wise clipping thresholds for the matrix coefficients. For square attention matrices ($n \times n$), the complexity of our TSC method is $O(n^2)$, which is linear in the number of matrix coefficients. We establish a theoretical bound on the discrepancy between TSC and the exact proximal projection and show on synthetic data that the additional Euclidean approximation cost remains small in practice. Extensive benchmarks demonstrate substantial runtime improvements over proximal baselines and validate the theoretical norm identities underlying the proposed construction.

Dual DeltaNet: Value-Side Memory Reorganization for Linear Attention

Authors: Yixiao Qian, Yaxin Zhang, Song Chen, Jiaxu Liu, Shengze Cai, Chao Xu | Date: 2026-10-28

Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38578v1

Abstract Linear attention and state space models (SSMs) offer efficient alternatives to softmax attention by maintaining a fixed-size recurrent state, but this design requires careful management to preserve useful historical associations and limit interference. The delta rule helps reduce interference through correction of the value associated with each key, but it does not explicitly account for how related value features are distributed across the state. We observe that a state $\boldsymbol{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_k\times d_v}$ can be viewed as a bank of $d_k$ addresses storing $d_v$-dimensional values. While a key-side read $\boldsymbol{S}^\top\boldsymbol{k}$ retrieves the value associated with $\boldsymbol{k}$, a value-side read $\boldsymbol{S}\boldsymbol{e}$ aggregates addresses whose stored value features match the selector $\boldsymbol{e}$. Based on this observation, we propose Dual DeltaNet (DDN), which extends Kimi Delta Attention (KDA) with a two-sided delta rule. The key-side delta rule reads with the current key to correct its associated value, while the complementary value-side delta rule reads with selected features of the current value to reorganize the addresses it occupies. We derive a chunkwise parallel training algorithm and a rank-2 recurrence that increases arithmetic intensity during inference. Compared with KDA, GDN-2, and Mamba-3, DDN achieves the strongest overall performance across language modeling, zero-shot downstream tasks, and retrieval, while incurring modest training and inference overhead relative to KDA. Co-Evolving Policy and Rubric via Capability Awareness for Open-Ended Generation

Authors: Chengsheng Zhang, Wenshen Xu, Yuchuan Wu, Zhiyuan Yu, Junfeng Fang, Tianlong Wang, Bingxue Qiu, Yongbin Li, Xiang Wang | Date: 2026-10-28

Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38566v1

Abstract Rubric-based reinforcement learning (RL) offers interpretable, fine-grained supervision for aligning large language models (LLMs) on open-ended generation tasks. However, static rubric criteria may become saturated or remain beyond the policy's exploration, rendering the reward signals insufficiently discriminative. While recent efforts seek to guide sampling exploration or evolve rubrics, they fail to explicitly factor in how rubric difficulty should track the evolving policy's capabilities, resulting in a persistent mismatch between the optimization objective and the policy's current capability frontier. In this paper, we propose \textbf{Co}-Evolving Policy and Rubric via \textbf{C}apability \textbf{A}wareness (\textbf{CoCA}), a closed-loop framework that jointly adapts the rubric and policy through criterion-level diagnosis. Specifically, CoCA contrasts query-only and rubric-guided rollouts to characterize each criterion relative to the current policy, recalibrates criteria with mismatched difficulty, and identifies guidance-revealed capability gaps for frontier-based learning. Based on this insight, CoCA either expands rubric-conditioned frontiers through guided optimization or transfers qualified gains through on-policy self-distillation. As the policy updates, CoCA improves the capability diagnosis and enables more reliable distillation, thereby completing the co-evolution loop. Extensive experiments across diverse benchmarks and LLMs demonstrate that CoCA consistently improves performance over rubric-based RL methods. Moreover, CoCA promotes the internalization of guidance-revealed capabilities into query-only behavior while mitigating capability degradation.

REVBRAIN: Real-time Probabilistic EEG Forecasting Using Residual Vector Quantization

Authors: Hanna Pankka, Jaakko Lehtinen, Timo Roine | Date: 2026-10-28

Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38558v1

Abstract Real-time forecasting of resting-state EEG signals is required by applications like brain-state-dependent stimulation, where computer generated actions are coupled with ongoing neural oscillations. Recently, the deep learning model WaveNet was proposed for the forecasting task. It shows superior accuracy compared to the prevailing method, the linear autoregressive (AR) model, and provides a built-in uncertainty measure due to its probabilistic modeling framework. However, inference using WaveNet is too slow for real-time use due to its sample-by-sample autoregressive operation: a single forecast requires evaluating the model tens or hundreds of times depending on the length of the prediction window. In this paper, we present REVBRAIN, a probabilistic deep learning method that produces forecasts in real time by generating the forecast over the whole prediction horizon at once. This is achieved by tokenizing the output space using residual vector quantization (RVQ) and generating forecasts over the items in the tokenized space. We tested REVBRAIN for forecasting 200 ms of EEG time series in the alpha-frequency band (8-13 Hz) and compared our results against WaveNet model. We found that REVBRAIN achieved real-time inference on a laptop GPU and an over $50\times$ speedup compared to WaveNet, while reaching superior prediction accuracy. By achieving real-time inference speed, REVBRAIN enables incorporating accurate and probabilistic EEG forecasting into BCI applications, opening up new ways for investigating the functioning of the human brain.

What Hallucinations Have We Measured? Semantic Illusion, Confabulation, and Expressed Uncertainty

Authors: Hazel H. Kim, Jakub Binkowski, Ekaterina Fadeeva, Juan David Guerra, Yijie Hao, Zheng Yi Ho, Baixiang Huang, Buyun Liang, Siyuan Liang, Anh Tuan Luu, Potsawee Manakul, Niels Mündler-Sasahara, Aidar Myrzakhan, Shahrad Mohammadzadeh, Artem Shelmanov, Kai Shu, Ponhvoan Srey, Dacheng Tao, René Vidal, Haofei Yu, Lei Yu, Hongxiang Zhang, Yarin Gal, Hinrich Schütze, Philip H. S. Torr | Date: 2026-10-28

Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38520v1

Abstract Hallucination remains one of the most critical challenges in deploying large language models (LLMs) reliably and safely. Yet the term is used inconsistently across the research community. We argue for a strict definition of hallucinations in LLMs. First, hallucinations should be confident incorrectness, kept separate from expressed uncertainty (UNK)1. UNK reveals that the model may be wrong, whereas a hallucination does not reveal it and is therefore more dangerous in practice. Conflating the two obscures their distinct behaviors and makes them hard to study. Second, hallucinations should be split by a simple re-elicitation test: does any semantically equivalent prompt recover the correct response? This test probes whether the relevant knowledge2 is accessible. If none does, the knowledge is likely absent or inaccessible, and the error type is a confabulation (CF) that likely requires external knowledge or retraining; if some does, the knowledge is accessible from the model but applied inconsistently, and the error type is a semantic illusion (SI) that simple prompt or decoding changes can mitigate3. Re-evaluating 14 recent hallucination methods under this taxonomy yields three findings. (1) Detection methods flag UNK more often than hallucinated responses (i.e., SI and CF). (2) Mitigation methods are far more effective for SI than for CF. (3) Latent signals are more informative than output-level signals for CF. An audit of recent hallucination papers shows that most conflate these categories. We recommend reporting CF, SI, and UNK separately, and introduce a practical labeling protocol that needs only a modest number of semantically equivalent prompts.

CoSTA: Co-Evolving Self-Play Timeline Agents for Complex Temporal Knowledge Graph Question Answering

Authors: Haikun Liu, Zhichao Yan, Shaoru Guo, Jiawei Gu, Victor Gutierrez Basulto, Jiapu Wang, Ru Li | Date: 2026-10-28

Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38450v1

Abstract Complex Temporal Knowledge Graph Question Answering (TKGQA) involves more than retrieving facts: the timestamp or validity interval of one fact can constrain the search for another, while multiple intervals may jointly determine the answer. Existing single-agent TKGQA methods typically investigate these facts within a single reasoning context, where evidence from different event histories can become interleaved and distractors can misdirect subsequent retrieval. Distributing investigations across agents can separate event-specific evidence into distinct contexts, but effective collaboration must still be learned because question decomposition, subtask investigation, and evidence synthesis are interdependent. To address these limitations, we propose \textbf{Co}-Evolving \textbf{S}elf-Play \textbf{T}imeline \textbf{A}gents (CoSTA), which organizes facts into navigable timelines, delegates investigations to workers, and uses a coordinator to synthesize their original timestamped observations. CoSTA initializes a shared policy through role-conditioned distillation and jointly optimizes both roles through co-evolving self-play with final-answer rewards. Across three benchmarks, CoSTA achieves state-of-the-art overall performance, with particularly strong gains on complex multi-fact questions, while its compact variants outperform the evaluated closed-source LLM agents.

Agent Transfer Protocol: Server-Mediated Agent-to-Agent Communication Architecture for the Agentic Web

Authors: Yuqi Qiu, Zuyao Xu, Lu Sun, Xiang Li, Fasheng Miao, Fubin Wu, Haixin Duan, Zheli Liu | Date: 2026-10-28

Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38398v1

Abstract The Agentic Web needs to support continuous collaboration among agents from different organizations. These collaborative relationships may span multiple tasks, network connections and execution instances. Such interactions require stable communication entries, message reception when instances are offline, and source verification after delayed reading. How to organize these responsibilities so that cross domain communication does not depend on specific execution instances being continuously online is a key issue for building long term agent interaction infrastructure. We propose ATP (Agent Transfer Protocol), a cross organization agent communication protocol adopting a domain mediated architecture. In this architecture, administrative domains maintain stable addresses and receiving entries for logical agents. The domain servers of both parties continuously undertake cross domain transmission. The receiving domain verifies and saves messages for execution instances to read later. The sending domain signs the complete message envelope. This allows the receiving instance to independently reverify the domain source and content integrity after the original connection ends, provided the original verification public key is still trusted and obtainable. We implemented an ATP prototype and evaluated it through a controlled cross organization LLM agent workflow, mechanism checks and latency measurements. The workflow completed subsequent business processing after instances went offline or were replaced. The independent verifier successfully verified the domain source and content integrity of saved messages. Latency measurements further quantified the communication and processing overhead introduced by the continuous mediation of domain servers.

Masked Erosion: How Acceptance Gates Lose Redundant Safeguards in Self-Improving Agents

Authors: Hau-Sing So, Daixian Liu, Weicai Long | Date: 2026-10-28

Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38392v1

Abstract Self-improving LLM agents edit their own harness, the prompts and rules around a fixed model, and keep an edit only if an acceptance gate scores it higher on a batch of tasks. Such a gate sees what the harness does under the current model, not what it would still prevent if one safeguard failed. When safeguards overlap, deleting one is masked: the others still block the harm, so the gate decides on task success alone and redundancy erodes unseen. We measure harm for every subset of four written safeguards in two agent environments and run 411 improvement loops under ten gate rules, mostly as controlled deletion experiments, plus three runs of the GEPA optimizer. A deletion behind another working safeguard adds almost no observable harm. A paired significance test blocks deletions that pass by chance but not deletions that genuinely raise task success, and a larger evaluation batch lets it delete cheaper safeguards: at the same cost, 20-task batches kept every harness protected while 40-task batches lost all six. Abstract Fine-tuning Large Language Models (LLMs) on instruction data can quietly erode their safety, even when no training example looks harmful. Because this safety-degrading data looks benign, standard content filters struggle to catch it. We tackle this problem by developing SimpleLoRIF, a scalable gradient embedding method that identifies fine-tuning examples whose gradients align with unsafe behavior. By simplifying a low-rank attribution method and restricting backpropagation to the final four transformer blocks, SimpleLoRIF can embed datasets on the order of a million examples, boosting gradient attribution from a small-scale analysis tool into a practical filter for full post-training datasets. Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37442v1

Abstract Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) inference on memory-constrained edge platforms is often dominated by expert-weight movement rather than computation. Although each token activates only a small subset of experts, routing is input-dependent, so all experts must remain accessible and memory movement dominates latency. Existing approaches such as uniform pruning and expert dropping overlook two properties of MoE execution: expert combinations are highly skewed in frequency, and experts contribute asymmetrically within each combination. We present CRISP, a combination-aware framework that co-designs pruning and runtime scheduling for efficient MoE serving on memory-constrained edge platforms. Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37434v1

Abstract A software feature usually evolves over time: developers extend its first version as new use cases and requirements arrive, and each extension builds on code written earlier. Most feature benchmarks evaluate a single request on a fixed codebase. Feature evolution requires evaluating successive extensions on an agent's own earlier code and checking whether the feature remains correct. We introduce FeatureArc to evaluate coding agents on evolving features, together with a pipeline that constructs its tasks from repository histories. The benchmark combines dependencies between successive stages of the same feature, explicit requirements and interface contracts, and executable checks across four programming languages. The pipeline validates fail-to-pass (F2P) tests for new behavior and pass-to-pass (P2P) tests for existing behavior on the reference code before and after each stage. Authors: Geunhyoung Kim, Sung Ju Hwang | Date: 2026-10-27

Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37386v1

Abstract Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models are bottlenecked by loading routed-expert weights from memory during interactive decoding at small batch sizes. Because most intermediate channels in a routed expert contribute little to any single token, channel selectors predict which channels matter and read only their weights, typically keeping channels above a threshold calibrated on offline data. We show that channel selection needs no calibration data. First, a calibrated threshold does not fix how much a selector reads, because the number of channels passing it varies across inputs; Prox with thresholds calibrated for an 18% read budget actually reads only 12.9% on average across our benchmarks. Second, top-k keeps each token's k highest-scoring channels and needs no calibration data. At the calibrated budget, a selector's top-k version matches or beats its threshold version on the same score in 40 of 43 comparisons at 13% and 18% budgets, including all 23 on generation tasks. The top-k versions also avoid the repetition-loop collapse that calibrated thresholds cause on Gemma 4. Calibrating a threshold thus provides neither of the two things selectors use it for: a known read budget and a consistent accuracy gain at that budget. We furthermore introduce a reader built entirely from the model's weights, which screens channels with a ternary sketch and recovers the skipped ones with a low-rank map. Across three post-trained MoE models, our reader retains 98.1% of full-model accuracy at 17.8% of routed-expert reads, with Triton kernels running MoE blocks 1.60 to 1.90x faster than optimized vLLM at batch one and up to 2.69x at larger batches on the same three models.

Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37369v2

Abstract Large language models (LLMs) are usually evaluated by final-output correctness or safety, yet an endpoint label does not reveal how a failure develops throughout the reasoning process. We therefore ask whether ordinary reasoning failures and adversarially induced failures can be characterized using the same internal coordinates. Towards this end, we in this paper study a white-box diagnostic framework CauTrace to disclose the root-cause of why LLM fails from three fundamental inspections: Functional States, Propagation of Representation, Temporal-State Coordination and Reasoning Trajectory. First, seven functional states are creatively defined to represent the support and constraint processing in LLMs. Second, the propagation of representation change measures how the changes among the seven states persist across subsequent Transformer blocks. Abstract Large language models (LLMs) require substantial memory, motivating low-bit weight quantization for efficient deployment. Existing vector quantization (VQ) methods often improve accuracy by increasing the effective coding dimension, which can complicate independent lookup and local codeword updates. We propose CoS-VQ (Coordinated Shared-Scale Vector Quantization), which preserves low-dimensional codewords while jointly optimizing hard indices and their shared scale across positions. RPSD: Retrieval-Privileged On-Policy Self-Distillation with Optimal Transport

Authors: Dongyue Li, Zebang Cheng, Zhongyi Deng, Hongboxu, Yi Bin, Fei Ma | Date: 2026-10-27

Link: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37281v1

Abstract Sequential recommendation predicts a user's next interaction from historical behavior. Recent LLM-based methods recast this task as generative recommendation and increasingly adopt reinforcement learning such as GRPO for post-training. However, these methods suffer from two limitations, namely credit-blind optimization, where a sequence-level reward is shared across all generated tokens, and collaborative-blind supervision, where each training signal is derived from a single user and ignores behavioral patterns shared across similar users. To address both issues, we propose Retrieval-Privileged on-policy Self-Distillation (RPSD). For credit-blind optimization, we introduce on-policy self-distillation (OPSD), in which a teacher with privileged knowledge re-evaluates the student's on-policy response, and the token-level confidence difference between the student and teacher reshapes the shared sequence-level advantage into token-specific credit. However, the privileged knowledge in vanilla OPSD is still derived from a single user, leaving collaborative-blind supervision unresolved. We therefore ground the teacher in collaborative behavioral evidence from similar users. Specifically, we use temporally aware optimal transport (OT) to compare interaction trajectories across users by jointly considering item similarity and temporal structure, and further apply diversity-aware selection to retain complementary trajectories as privileged evidence. In this way, on-policy self-distillation provides fine-grained token-level credit, while OT enriches the teacher with structured cross-user evidence. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to bring on-policy self-distillation to sequential recommendation and to use cross-user behavior as its privileged signal. Abstract Proprietary language models and their reasoning traces are vulnerable to API-based distillation, where an adversary trains a student model on the teacher model's outputs. To obtain evidence of distillation, model providers can watermark the teacher's outputs, aiming to leave a radioactive signal in the student's outputs. Existing black-box detectors search for such radioactivity using token-level statistics of the suspect model's outputs.
