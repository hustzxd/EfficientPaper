## PSGAgent: Enhancing MLLMs' Dynamic Visual Perception for Panoptic Scene Graph Generation via Agentic Reasoning

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/pipeline-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/LLM-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/LLMs-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/language model-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Qingyun Liu, Yuankun Liu, Jiaye Feng, Jie Huang, Zonghong Dai, Haochen Li, Jingkun Wang, Weiping Li, Tong Mo | **Date:** 2026-11-05

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07408v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07408v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Panoptic Scene Graph generation (PSG) requires holistic yet fine-grained reasoning over complex relations among objects. However, current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) applied to PSG suffer from a perception bottleneck: they function as static observers limited by fixed-resolution views. As a result, models often overlook critical relations involving small or peripheral objects and struggle to differentiate ambiguous predicates in complex scenes. To address these limitations, we propose PSGAgent, an agentic framework that enhances visual perception for PSG through a think-act-observe loop. PSGAgent allows MLLMs to dynamically refine their visual perception using two tools: a Region Zoom-In Tool for local exploration of small or boundary relations and a Visual Highlight Tool for relation-focused segmentation that filters out irrelevant contextual noise. To optimize decision-making, we construct a two-stage training pipeline. First, we create a high-quality, error-aware Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (M-CoT) dataset that provides robust supervision for cold-start learning. Then, we design a novel hierarchical reward mechanism integrated with Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) to enable strategic and adaptive tool usage. Extensive experiments on the PSG benchmark show that PSGAgent achieves SOTA performance, demonstrating strong capability in both holistic and fine-grained scene understanding.

</details>

## Rigorously Certified Neural-Network Computation of Nonlinear Water Waves at Near-Machine Precision

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/pipeline-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/language model-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Michal Shavit, Yongji Wang, Tristan Buckmaster | **Date:** 2026-11-05

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07395v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07395v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present a computational framework that combines machine-learning-based discovery with rigorous computer-assisted numerical certification and formal verification for strongly nonlinear partial differential equations. We demonstrate the approach for periodic gravity-capillary traveling water waves. Physics-informed neural networks are used as a search mechanism, with the wave profile, wave speed and, in continuation experiments, surface tension learned directly from the governing equations without training data. Through a staged training and refinement procedure, the neural networks achieve near-machine-precision residuals of the governing equations, even in strongly nonlinear regimes. A single formulation recovers high-amplitude Stokes waves, Crapper waves and Wilton ripples. Continuation from a high-amplitude Wilton ripple in surface tension reveals new high-amplitude candidate solutions. We freeze one candidate as an explicit finite representation and rigorously certify a uniform bound on its governing-equation residual using interval arithmetic, accounting for floating-point roundoff, Fourier truncation, aliasing and grid-to-continuum errors. This independently validates the neural-network accuracy. We formalize the certification argument in Lean, which machine-checks the deductions from certified numerical bounds to the residual theorem. Both the interval certification and formalization were developed with substantial assistance from a large language model, while the validity of the result remains independent of the language model: the numerical bounds are rigorously enclosed by interval arithmetic and the subsequent mathematical deductions are checked by the Lean kernel. Together, these steps provide a pipeline from neural discovery to rigorous numerical certification and machine-checked verification with auditable mathematical guarantees.

</details>

## CacheGraft: Enhancing Retrieval-Augmented Generation via Latent-Space Document Enrichment

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/KV cache-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/communication-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/transformer-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/inference-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/language model-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Vihan Lakshman, Peter Baile Chen, Zhuohan Gu, Michael Cafarella | **Date:** 2026-11-05

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07393v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07393v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Recent work in machine learning has demonstrated that transformer-based language model agents can communicate state not only through text, but also directly in latent space via key-value (KV) caches. In this paper, we argue that such cache-to-cache translation can provide utility beyond the original motivating application of real-time agent communication and can instead be utilized for retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). Building on the Cache-to-Cache (C2C) fusion framework, our proposed system CacheGraft encodes each corpus passage offline into a fused KV cache representation between a larger sharer model (4B parameters in our experiments) and a smaller receiver model (0.6B parameters) such that this cache can be injected into the receiver at query time with no need for the larger model. Across five QA benchmarks and two independent locally-run judges, CacheGraft improves answer quality over the text-only receiver baseline in 29 out of 30 comparisons, of which 17 of the 30 are statistically significant. Notably, these gains are zero-shot with respect to the fuser, which was never trained on retrieval data. These initial results suggest that latent-space document enrichment may be a promising avenue for enhancing the performance of RAG systems, particularly in compute-constrained settings where it is only feasible to deploy a small model at inference time.

</details>

## WeLM-Image: A Unified Image Generation Model towards Visual Intelligence

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/sparse-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/attention-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/distillation-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/transformer-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Canmiao Fu, Chen Li, Guohui Zhang, Hao Lu, Hongyang Wei, Huadong Wu, Huaisong Zhang, Hubery Yin, Liyuan Cui, Qixin Yan, Tong Shao, Xiaoqiang Zhou, Xinhang Leng, Ying Zhang, Zhenhai Liu, Zhipeng Huang | **Date:** 2026-11-05

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07392v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07392v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present \textbf{WeLM-Image}, a unified image foundation model that integrates multimodal understanding into text-to-image generation, instruction-guided editing, and multi-reference synthesis. Designed for single-GPU deployment in WeChat's large-scale consumer applications, the model processes text, reference images, and target latents as one interleaved sequence. A Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) backbone combines shared attention with independent understanding and generation parameters to limit optimization interference. Equipping each path with Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) modules provides sparse capacity, with 31.7B stored parameters and approximately 5.4B generation parameters activated per target latent token. A generation-oriented data engine combines a knowledge-grounded visual vocabulary with large-scale visual clustering to balance semantic coverage and editing tasks, while training-time evaluation guides iterative data refinement. Bilingual annotations and verified editing supervision support progressive training up to native 1.5K resolution. We optimize generation and editing separately with rubric- and checklist-based rewards, then consolidate the specialized policies through noise-aware on-policy distillation. In the reported overall comparisons, the 1536-resolution model ranks first among the evaluated open-source models on PRISM-Bench, Qwen-Image-Bench, and UnicBench. MeanFlow-based few-step distillation yields WeLM-Image-Turbo, which generates a $1024\times1024$ image in 4.98\,s on a single NVIDIA H20 GPU using eight guidance-free steps.

## Last-Iterate Convergence of Stochastic Gradient Methods for Unbounded Problems

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/attention-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/inference-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Kevin Kurian Thomas Vaidyan, Ahmet Alacaoglu, Michael P. Friedlander | **Date:** 2026-11-05

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07334v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07334v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Last-iterate convergence guarantees for stochastic gradient methods have attracted significant attention as practical implementations typically return the last iterate instead of an average of the iterates. In this paper, we study two approaches for analyzing the last-iterate convergence of proximal and projected stochastic gradient methods without bounded domain or bounded variance assumptions, where black-box variational inference serves as a running example. Under two related assumptions relaxing the bounded variance, we show near-optimal anytime rate $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{T})$ for the last iterate. Moreover, when the horizon $T$ is known, we remove the logarithmic factor and obtain the optimal order $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{T})$, matching the order known under bounded variance or bounded domain assumptions.

## WeightLoop: Weight-Space Programming with Experimental Feedback

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/recursive self-improvement-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/self-improvement-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/LLM-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/serving-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/inference-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/language model-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Mengran Li, Bo Li, Weijing Zhao, Chengyang Zhang, Boyu Zhang, Jinfeng Xu, Zelin Zang, Victor C. M. Leung, Stan Z. Li | **Date:** 2026-11-05

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07319v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07319v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Weight-space programming (WSP) shows that language models can reason about and rewrite neural network weights; recursive self-improvement (RSI) motivates feedback-driven improvement. Models guiding experiments receive concrete feedback: completed assays reveal prediction errors and inform what to test next. We introduce WeightLoop, a framework that turns experimental feedback into persistent updates to deployed predictors. GPT-6 Astra inspects a task specification and the model implementation, then writes a bounded, reusable parameter-editing program. As new measurements arrive, the program identifies prediction discrepancies, validates candidate edits using observed outcomes, and writes the selected changes back to the original model. The editor remains fixed while the predictor evolves, enabling repeated updates without further LLM calls or gradient retraining while preserving the original inference interface. In offline replay, targeted edits improve mean ranking utility in seven of eight endpoint-budget settings (all eight versus random edits) and mean final discovery across three sequential tasks. WeightLoop connects WSP with an RSI-inspired improvement loop, making experimental evidence a direct driver of model change and subsequent decisions.

## Encoding and Propagation of Grammatical Number in Large Language Models

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/sparse-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/LLM-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/LLMs-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/language model-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Jacob Solinsky, Serguei Pakhomov | **Date:** 2026-11-05

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07283v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07283v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) have become an increasingly popular means of translating Large Language Model (LLM) hidden state vectors into a human-comprehensible list of features or symbols. As such, SAEs may provide insight into an LLM's knowledge representation and internal ``mental'' grammar - topics of pragmatic interest for mechanistic interpretation of AI behavior and of theoretical interest for the study of in silico representations of human language. Language models with fewer than 100M parameters, trained on small corpora, nonetheless exhibit strong grammatical competence and discourse coherence, despite producing content of limited reliability. We use SAEs that we trained on one such model (Pythia-70M) to conduct an in-depth examination of how a single grammatical phenomenon, number agreement, is represented and processed in an LLM. We demonstrate the mechanism and the methodology by which SAEs can be used to find and propagate ``plural noun'', ``plural matrix subject'', and ``generate a plural verb'' features which cause the LLM to produce plural verb forms such as ``are'', ``do'', or ``have'' instead of ``is'', ``does'', and ``has'' in response to prompts such as ``the men in the park''. We show that intervening to remove these features from the hidden states of plural subject noun tokens causes the model to produce singular verbs instead, and vice versa. We also show that corresponding ``singular noun'' and ``generate a singular verb'' features are not as effective for this task. This asymmetry suggests that Pythia-70m makes use of a privative representation of grammatical number in English. These findings replicated with larger LLMs Gemma-2-2b and Llama-3.1-8b yielded similar results. Our main contribution is a novel approach to the use of SAEs to understanding how LLMs encode human language that addresses a number of technical and methodological challenges.

</details>

## Energy Measurement and Optimization of TinyML in Resource-Constrained Embedded Systems: A Systematic Mapping Study

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The rapid expansion of the Internet of Things (IoT) has increased the volume of data generated at the network edge, where many devices operate under severe memory, processing, and energy constraints. In this context, Tiny Machine Learning (TinyML) enables machine learning (ML) tasks to be executed on microcontrollers, smart sensors, and other resource-constrained platforms. However, the literature remains heterogeneous regarding the algorithms evaluated, energy-measurement methods, reported metrics, influencing factors, and optimization strategies. This study presents a systematic mapping of energy-aware TinyML research. The optimization strategies were grouped into model compression, hardware-aware design, system and communication optimization, and data and processing optimization. The results also reveal limited standardization in experimental platforms, measurement procedures, and reporting practices, limiting reproducibility and direct comparison across studies. This mapping provides an updated overview of the field and guidance for developing and evaluating more energy-efficient TinyML solutions.

## Using Transformers for Teaching and Learning via Symbolic Distillation of Neural A-life Agents

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/distillation-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/transformer-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Shuwei Wang, Vadim Bulitko, Matthew R. G. Brown, Michael Youngblood, Ramon Lawrence, Roscoe Smith, Shinichi Nakagawa, William Yeoh | **Date:** 2026-11-05

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07254v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07254v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Neural networks controlling behavior of A-life agents can evolve over multiple generations. While effective in making the agents survive, such networks are generally opaque to a human observer. Furthermore, the neural weights cannot be transferred between agents with different neural architectures. Distilling such neural networks into compact symbolic policies can help humans understand agent behavior in A-life. Additionally, it allows the agents to share their knowledge in a compact fashion, improving population-level learning. Distilling a neural policy into a symbolic policy is typically performed by searching the space of symbolic policies to find one that approximates the neural policy. However, even with a domain-specific language the space of policies can be large, necessitating incomplete stochastic search such as genetic algorithms. Yet the distillation remains computationally expensive. We propose to instead use a transformer pre-trained on readily available random percept-action data. Doing so speeds up distillation substantially. We derive the transformer architecture via a neural architecture search. Empirical results compare different distillation methods and the influence of the symbolic space on A-life population dynamics through self-reflection and teaching and learning.

</details>

## How Do AI Agents Waste Your Money?

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/pruning-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/LLM-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/serving-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Huimin Zeng, Yuxuan Lu, Jesse Cai, Eric Hanchen Jiang, Di Wu, Po-Nien Kung, Wenbo Hu, Ziyi Wang, Dakuo Wang, Jiaxin Pei, Kai-Wei Chang | **Date:** 2026-11-04

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07238v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07238v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

AI agents may consume massive tokens across a wide range of tasks, but how many of those tokens are really necessary? Although prior work has shown that agents can incur unexpectedly high token costs, we still lack a systematic way to identify and measure token waste. In this work, we introduce a unified framework for measuring token waste across heterogeneous agent systems. Our framework records and normalizes complete agent trajectories, identifies removable spans using an LLM-as-a-Judge (LLMaJ), and maps the identified text spans back to the original trajectory for token-level measurement. We validate the identified waste through direct interventions by removing marked spans, resuming agent execution, and measuring changes in task outcomes and subsequent actions. In online pruning experiments, we remove 41-46% of the model's reasoning while largely preserving task performance across two distinct agent benchmarks. Using this framework, we analyze agents across multiple settings. Our analysis shows that token waste has a system-level structure that aggregate token usage alone cannot reveal. First, waste varies more across tasks and agent settings than across models. Second, reasoning and visible execution can exhibit different waste patterns. Third, different harnesses can introduce distinct sources of inefficiency even under the same model and task. Overall, our framework transforms token waste into an actionable optimization signal, enabling researchers and practitioners to train more efficient models, design better reasoning strategies, and optimize agent harnesses toward reducing unnecessary computation rather than merely reducing token usage.

</details>

## Loopback Transformer: Towards a Unified View of Residual Looped Transformers

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/attention-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/transformer-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Xuan Ouyang, Zefan Cai, Chenlong Zhang, Cheng Luo, Ke Wan, Junjie Hu | **Date:** 2026-11-04

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07226v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07226v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Looped Transformers reduce parameter count by repeatedly applying shared blocks, but parameter sharing alone does not determine how the results of repeated computation are reused. We take the depth-memory view of residual connections and analyze which representation is stored after each block application and how later reads weight it. A full block output contains both its input and its new update, so weighting that output also reweights the history already carried by the input. Reading individual updates instead, with the shared stack's input kept on a separate path, gives separate controls over input strength, total update weight, and allocation across updates. The Loopback Transformer implements these controls: geometric decay sets the total update weight and supplies a recency prior, and attention learns how to allocate that weight. On ClimbMix, Loopback reaches lower validation loss than standard Transformer baselines with 40-75% of their Transformer parameters (excluding token embeddings and the output head) at reference scales from 191M to 3B, with training tokens and executed depth matched. It also has higher mean accuracy over thirteen downstream tasks at 1.4B and 3B. At 191M, 374M, and 778M, it has the lowest validation loss and the highest thirteen-task mean among the standard Transformer and seven looped residual designs.

</details>

## TileQuant: Tile as the Precision-Allocation Unit for LLM Mixed-Precision Post-Training Quantization

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/quantization-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/quantize-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/decoding-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/LLM-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/serving-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/language model-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Hanzhi Zhang, Qiao Zhang, Qinglei Cao, Heng Fan, Yan Huang, Kewei Sha, Yunhe Feng | **Date:** 2026-11-04

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07217v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07217v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Post-training quantization (PTQ) reduces large language model (LLM) deployment costs, but preserving quality requires accounting for uneven weight sensitivity. Uniform PTQ uses one weight bit-width, importance-aware PTQ adjusts scales or reconstructs weights within that width, and column-wise mixed precision retains selected columns at higher precision. However, sensitivity varies within columns, so whole-column retention also protects less sensitive weights, while runtime outlier detection adds decoding overhead. We therefore introduce TileQuant, a training-free framework that assigns precision to hardware-aligned 2D weight tiles. Offline calibration provides input-activation-aware scoring from forward statistics for lower calibration cost and gradient-informed scoring from loss sensitivity for stronger quality preservation. Within each projection's retention budget, the highest-scoring tiles remain at 16-bit and the rest use 8-bit. The resulting map guides reconstruction into compact weight buffers, which the kernel reuses for native integer and 16-bit computation with local accumulation to reduce memory traffic. Experiments show that TileQuant reaches 2.66x the original model's generation throughput. Across language modeling, reasoning, long-context retrieval, and question answering, it preserves quality and surpasses quantized and original models on multiple tasks. These results support selecting precision units that capture local sensitivity while enabling efficient hardware execution. The implementation is: https://github.com/HanzhiZhang-Ulrica/TileQuant

</details>

## Compositional Unalignment: Auditing Safety in Large Vision-Language Models

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/pipeline-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/LLM-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/language model-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Hong kyu Lee, Kai Shu, Li Xiong | **Date:** 2026-11-04

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07216v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07216v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large vision-language models (LVLMs) are vulnerable to compositional harm: an image and a prompt that are benign in isolation become harmful through the model's cross-modal reasoning. Recent alignment methods report strong scores on compositional safety benchmarks, but an LLM judge only measures observed behavior and cannot reveal how robustly safety is integrated into the model. We argue that un-alignment provides a complementary audit of alignment robustness. We first show that minimal supervised fine-tuning reaches an LLM-judge score comparable to a multi-stage reinforcement-learning pipeline, so similar benchmark scores cannot establish robustness by themselves. We then show that recognition and refusal of compositional harm are decoupled: nine standard direction-finding methods detect compositional harm with up to AUC=0.99, yet ablating the recovered directions either leaves refusal intact or destroys model coherence. We therefore propose CMaG, a training-free un-alignment method for compositional safety. CMaG measures refusal intention with a refusal-versus-compliance log-probability margin, takes its gradient with respect to mid-layer activations, and contrasts it against partially benign pairs to isolate the cross-modal interaction term. A single backward pass over 80 examples yields a direction along which steering monotonically converts refusal into compliance. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B and LLaVA-1.5-7B, CMaG reduces compositional safety scores by up to 65 points while over 98% of outputs remain coherent, outperforming gradient-based baselines; the direction also transfers to text-explicit harm and un-aligns models hardened by defensive training. Compositional safety can thus appear behaviorally strong while remaining fragile to targeted internal intervention. Warning: this paper contains unsafe model outputs, including references to self-harm.

</details>

## Capability Gain from ISA to Shared Execution on AMD MI300A and MI250X

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Each new GPU generation is showcased by its peak instruction rates, yet users hardly ever run instructions directly: they reach the hardware's performance through vendor libraries, applications and devices shared with other jobs. How much of a theoretical speedup survives these layers is rarely measured, so a team deciding whether to move to a new generation, or to adopt FP8 or 2:4 sparsity, lacks adequate guidance. ## FastAFD: When Does Attention-FFN Disaggregation Pay on Rack-Scale NVLink?

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/attention-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/overlap-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/decoding-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/disaggregation-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/speculative-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/parallelism-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/LLM-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/vLLM-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/serving-FF8C00)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Attention-FFN disaggregation (AFD) separates attention from the experts of a mixture-of-experts (MoE) model and has been reported to raise decode throughput over colocated serving. The model yields a concise insight: AFD's speedup factors into a batch gain (larger batches, since attention GPUs hold no expert weights), an overlap gain (shorter steps, since the MoE runs on the expert GPUs behind attention), and a disaggregation cost (the GPUs given to experts). AFD has the advantage when the two gains outweigh the cost. This view explains which models benefit from AFD, and shows that speculative decoding helps light-expert models most, where the enlarged overlap gain outweighs the larger disaggregation cost and adds a further 8% to AFD's advantage over wide EP. FastAFD is open source at https://github.com/hao-ai-lab/FastAFD.

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/pruning-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/pipeline-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/inference-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/language model-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Georgios Feretzakis, Alexandros Papaspyridis | **Date:** 2026-11-04

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06498v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06498v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Offline retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) on edge devices is updated through signed knowledge packs that carry text and precomputed embedding vectors. A signature proves who published a pack, not that its vectors encode its text. We study this text-vector integrity gap. A verifier that recomputes vectors and accepts them within a cosine tolerance concedes a predictable share of queries to an attacker who controls the vectors: the hijack rate equals the distribution of angular retrieval margins at the tolerance (the margin law). Margins are small in five corpora and two embedding models, so an idealised tolerance at the level of floating-point noise leaves up to 5.5% of queries exposed (at most 1.2% in any corpus once exact duplicate documents are excluded), and a threshold calibrated from our CPU INT8-FP32 comparison of a small embedding model (at the empirical 0.1% quantile of honest cosines) leaves 98-100% exposed; on a Raspberry Pi 5 running a local language model, such an attack lowers correct answers from 0.75 to 0.39. Integrity audits of compressed vector stores fare no better: on a FAISS product-quantisation index over 57,638 documents, tampered vectors that all pass the audit put the attacker's document first for 25% of targets. Verifying quantisation codes instead, with ambiguity decided by the verifier, a calibrated guard band and canonicalisation, accepted every honest vector in our samples on three platforms and left the publisher no choice. We also show that common floating-point and dynamic-INT8 inference pipelines are not bit-reproducible across batch sizes or architectures, and report pre-registered negative results on abstention, context pruning and semantic screening. We release code and data.

</details>

## ArcRAG: Agentic Compositional Retrieval over Knowledge Graphs

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/pipeline-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/inference-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Jyotin Goel, Abhinav Arun, Tejas Prakash Agarwal, Bhaskarjit Sarmah, Stefano Pasquali | **Date:** 2026-11-04

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Graph-based retrieval-augmented generation (GraphRAG) answers questions by retrieving over a knowledge graph built from a corpus. ## Hardwired Distributed Processing Units for Inference: Neural Decisions at the Sensor that Free the Host

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/pipeline-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/inference-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Raj Mali | **Date:** 2026-11-04

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06436v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06436v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We show that a hardwired distributed processing unit (HDPU) can take a neural decision at the sensor and so remove the inference from the host. Built for zebra-crossing detection on a low-cost Artix-7 FPGA, the unit decides on every camera frame in a fixed 2,269,477 clock cycles (34.04 ms depends upon Fpga clk ), inside the 37.49-ms frame period, and on the board it returns the same scores and decisions as its bit-exact software model on all 639 test images. The unit is an 80x60-pixel pipeline feeding a four-layer binarized CNN with 93,408 one-bit weights, verified bit-exactly from a Python model through RTL simulation to the board. Earlier HDPUs did pre-processing (edges, corners, steering); this one shows that the decision itself can move to the sensor.

## CoRTeC: A Differentially Private Release for Synthetic Tabular Data, Decodable by Any Generator

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/overlap-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/overlapping-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/inference-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/language model-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Calister Nnona | **Date:** 2026-11-04

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06342v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06342v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The records that could train the most useful models, such as a hospital's patient files, are often the ones an institution cannot share. We propose CoRTeC, a method that releases synthetic versions of such records under differential privacy. Differential privacy places a proven limit on what the synthetic data can reveal about any person, and in particular on membership inference. CoRTeC spends the privacy budget once, on a small file of noisy counts designed for training a classifier. For each group of records, the file gives the group's size, its balance of outcomes, a histogram of every column for each outcome and the outcome rate in each of several non-overlapping cells. A reviewer can check every number before the file leaves the institution. Anyone can then turn the counts into synthetic rows, as often as they like and at no further privacy cost. A maximum-entropy decoder can do this without any language model, and so can each of the five frozen language models we tested. None of them ever sees a real record. A selection step then matches the rows to the released counts. On NHANES and bank marketing, without the selection step, it stays within 0.05 AUC. On Adult, with a fixed cohort rule, the release carries the outcome rates a deployment names more closely than a real sample does, at some cost in accuracy on pairs of columns. Four controls show that most of the utility comes from the release and the selection step, and that a language model adds what it knows from the column names. No private row ever reaches a model, and no row CoRTeC generates copies a real one. The strongest membership attack stays far below the bound the budget allows.

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06122v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06122v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Molecular dynamics predicts molecular motion from atomic forces and trajectory integration. Density functional theory (DFT) provides first-principles forces, but its $O(N^3)$ cost limits simulations to thousands of atoms; empirical force fields scale to millions but miss bond breaking and charge transfer. In a live $10.24$M-atom salt solution, NVE energy drift is $0.42\,μ$eV/atom/ps over $10$ ps, and structure matches a serial reference. A 10.06M-atom Norovirus capsid sustains 100 ps of NVT at $\approx 8$ ps/day on 256 A100 GPUs. Over 200,000 steps, it has no broken bonds or anomalously close pairs and uses three compiled variants, with no graph breaks or eager fallbacks.

## Shallow Encoding Emits Deep Memories

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/attention-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/prefill-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/low-rank-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/serving-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/compression-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/language model-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Mingyuan Ma, Yangmin Li, Harris Nover, Zheyu Fu, Po-Han Huang, Alex Settle, Ashwin Nanjappa | **Date:** 2026-11-04

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06084v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06084v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Hybrid language models interleave a few softmax-attention layers with many linear-attention or state-space layers, so every layer keeps a memory of earlier tokens: a key-value cache or a fixed-size recurrent state. Serving them spends more compute and high-bandwidth memory on these memories than the output requires. The prefill runs every layer for every prompt token, and every decode step loads and stores whole recurrent states, which on Qwen3.8-Flash-Next take as many bytes per request as the attention cache of thousands of tokens. We find two properties of trained hybrids that locate this excess. First, writes are easier than reads: writing a deep layer's memory of a token needs little of the deep computation that predicting the next token needs. Trained copies of the deep layers' write paths, which we call emitters, therefore write a prompt's deep memories from the residual stream of a shallower anchor layer, and all prompt tokens but the last run as few as 63% of the layers. Second, recurrent states have a state sink: most of their energy lies in a rank-one term along a fixed direction of each head, and the rest is a low-rank working memory. SEED builds on both: it makes the prefill shallow in depth and the decode state shallow in representation. Each recurrent state is stored as its sink plus low-rank factors, which the Gated DeltaNet, Kimi Delta Attention and Mamba-2 updates extend exactly; we prove that the open-loop error of the periodic compressions back to low rank is bounded independently of their number. SEED keeps the original weights frozen, compresses no attention cache, and runs all layers for every generated token. On six checkpoints of five production hybrids of 30B to over 300B parameters, it is nearly lossless on eleven official exams. Served with SGLang on NVIDIA GB300 GPUs on an agentic-coding trace, it raises the per-GPU throughput of Qwen3.8-Flash-Next 1.2-1.4x.

## TauLang: A Language and Compiler for Space-Time Programming on Statically Scheduled Accelerators

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/scheduling-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/inference-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/language model-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Xuxiao Yang, Junchi Wu, Feiyang Wu, Zhuoran Li, Shuo Tao, Dongyi Fan, Yiming Zhang, Keqin Luo, Xingrui Gao, Xuehai Qian, Youwei Zhuo | **Date:** 2026-11-04

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06079v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06079v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Statically scheduled accelerators such as Groq's Tensor Streaming Processor (TSP) achieve low-latency large language model inference by stripping dynamic synchronization hardware from the chip. We present TauLang, a kernel language and compiler that makes time a first-class citizen of the program. On a cycleaccurate model of a TSP-like machine, joint search achieves a $1.40\times$ latency speedup across ten kernels over a space-first flow. A few explicit time pins keep latency within one cycle of the untimed schedules and scale joint search to 1,636 GEMM blocks, whereas untimed search finds no schedule at 32.

It combines partitioned bulk extraction, declarative mapping templates, rule-based and statistical quality gates, master data management with deterministic and blocked fuzzy matching, constrained loading with per-record error capture, and change data capture guarded by a data-health circuit breaker. Each stage is mapped to Jitterbit Integration Studio constructs. The framework was implemented as an open reference prototype and evaluated on synthetic two-organization scenarios with known ground truth at three scales, from 32,040 to 383,973 source records, over five random seeds. At a preset threshold, hybrid matching reached pairwise F1 scores of 0.918 for accounts and 0.885 for contacts; calibrating the threshold per object would raise these to 0.938 and 0.963. The quality gates prevented all load failures and all silent loss of unmapped picklist values without wrongly flagging any clean record, whereas removing them produced about 1,000 load failures per run. Over five simulated days of hourly incremental synchronization, replayed events were ignored, an injected upstream fault was contained in the batch in which it occurred, and the target agreed with a full rebuild on more than 99.3 percent of field values. Validation on a production deployment remains future work.

## CHARM+: Cross-Hardware Attention with Re-Merge Multistream Mechanism

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/attention-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/communication-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/overlap-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/parallelism-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/LLM-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/LLMs-FF8C00) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/language model-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Xinhang Zhang, Boning Zhang, Chengchun Liu, Chunpu Li, Lei Sun, Weifeng Zhang, Limin Xiao | **Date:** 2026-11-04

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05906v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05906v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

As sequence lengths continue to grow, distributed attention based on sequence parallel (e.g., Ring-Attention) has become a mainstream solution for large language models (LLMs). Nevertheless, inherent communication dependencies limit computation-communication overlap, reducing GPU utilization. Moreover, in cross-hardware (NUMA or node) architectures, due to imbalanced communication bandwidth, frequent communication synchronization will limit overall performance. Based on these insights, we propose CHARM+, Cross-Hardware Attention with Re-Merge Multistream Mechanism. We reorganize sequential communications into several levels and achieve intra-level parallelism and computation overlap. Furthermore, we decouple cross-hardware communication from intra-hardware communication, eliminating synchronization overhead caused by bandwidth imbalance. Forward and backward algorithms were evaluated on multiple hardware architectures and consistently outperformed state-of-the-art methods. Compared to Ring-Attention, our forward and backward algorithms achieve average speedups of 1.3x and 1.7x on a dual-NUMA PCIe architecture, and 2.8x and 2.9x on a dual-node NVLink architecture, respectively.

## RECAP: Context-Aware Prediction Recycling with Request-Local and Persistent Memories for Multimodal Speculative Decoding

![](https://img.shields.io/badge/decoding-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/speculative-F08080) ![](https://img.shields.io/badge/language model-FF8C00)

**Authors:** Hengjie Zhu, Dayan Wu, Xinze Liu, Peng Fu, Zheng Lin, Weiping Wang | **Date:** 2026-11-04

**Link:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05894v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05894v1)

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Recycling a target model's own predictions offers a training-free source of proposals for speculative decoding. In vision-language models, however, existing recycling indexes predictions by the last token alone, conflating predictions computed under different images and prefixes; we find that such reuse fails most often at image-sensitive decoding states. Longer keys reduce this aliasing but match fewer states. We introduce \method, which stores target predictions under one-, two-, and three-token keys: contextual memories live only for the current request and are thus implicitly conditioned on its image, while token-level memory persists across requests to retain coverage. At each lookup, entropy-confidence weighting fuses the retrieved predictions according to their key specificity and concentration. Best-first search composes the fused transitions into a candidate tree, supplemented by suffix-matched continuations, and one target pass verifies the tree under greedy decoding. Without training or an auxiliary draft model, \method\ achieves mean speedups over autoregressive decoding of $1.66\speed$ and $1.56\speed$ across seven single-turn benchmarks on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and LLaVA-1.6-7B, exceeding the strongest training-free baseline, Token Recycling ($1.51\speed$ and $1.37\speed$). Our implementation is available in https://github.com/Senmo996/RECAP-official-code.

</details>
